Shared Pool Normalization

By default, CDI for each system is computed within its own normalization pool. A “High CDI” in the target system is relative to that system only, which may not correspond to the same absolute conditions as in the calibration system.

When checked, a single TPI analysis covers both calibration and demand systems together. All routes are scored relative to the same pool of geographies, making CDI directly comparable across systems. The calibration is automatically refitted using the new shared-pool CDI values for calibration routes.

Use when: calibrating against one peer system and applying to a target with substantially different absolute density levels.

Warning: Changing feature selections changes the normalization pool and may shift all CDI values. Re-run after any changes.

Corridor Demand Index (CDI)

A composite score (1–5) summarizing transit demand potential across all census geographies within the corridor buffer.

How it’s calculated: The CDI is a population-weighted average of the Transit Propensity Index (TPI) scores for each block group or tract in the study area. TPI scores combine nine factors—population density, employment density, zero-vehicle households, poverty, seniors, disability, people of color, youth, and limited English proficiency—each ranked by quintile within the corridor (1 = lowest, 5 = highest).

What it means: A CDI of 4–5 indicates strong latent demand for transit. Scores are relative to your study corridor only, not a national benchmark.

Why scores cluster: Because quintile scores are bounded (1–5) and each route covers many block groups of varying character, the population-weighted average naturally regresses toward 3. The default 70% weight on density (population + employment) means density variation is the primary differentiator. Adjust weights in the Calibrate tab to emphasize other factors.
